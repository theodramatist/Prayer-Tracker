Prayer Tracker for the Eastern Ridges

A daily prayer list for the clergy of the Eastern Ridges, with each other on it, the Diocese of Wisconsin’s cycles of prayer, the Anglican Cycle of Prayer, the people of your church, your own prayer requests, and the Collect of the Day from the Book of Common Prayer, in Rite One or Rite Two.

The address

theodramatist.github.io/Prayer-Tracker/eastern-ridges/ Open Prayer Tracker

On an iPhone or iPad

  1. Open the address above in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow pointing up).
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  4. Open Prayers from your Home Screen. It works like any other app, even with no connection.

On an Android phone

  1. Open the address above in Chrome.
  2. Tap ⋮ (top right), then Install app or Add to Home screen.
  3. Open Prayers from your Home screen or app drawer.

The Eastern Ridges clergy

  • The Eastern Ridges Clergy list has everyone in the region, to pray for every day, in order of last name.
  • To take your own name off: tap Lists at the bottom, then Eastern Ridges Clergy; tap your name, then Delete. (Undo, on the message that appears, puts it back.) Or, when your name comes up on Today, tap it and choose Delete.
  • To add someone: tap +, choose Eastern Ridges Clergy, and type their name, First Last.
  • To pray for them a day at a time instead, or under another name for the list: Lists → Eastern Ridges Clergy → ⋯.

Your church’s list

  • On the first screen, under My Church, type your church’s name.
  • Then tap Paste names and paste in your parish list, one name per line (First Last, or Last, First). A spreadsheet exported from your church’s directory (with First Name and Last Name columns) works too. Choose Spread A–Z to share them out over the days of the week, or put a day’s name (Monday, Tuesday…) on its own line above that day’s names.
  • Or tap Add one to add people one at a time.
  • To add someone later, tap +, choose your church’s list, and type their name.
  • To pray for everyone every day instead of a day at a time: Lists → your church → ⋯ → turn on Everyone, every day.

Lesser Feasts and Fasts

  1. In the app, tap Lists at the bottom, then the Settings button at the top right (the sliders), then go down to Lesser Feasts and Fasts.
  2. Tap Download the PDF from the Episcopal Church. On an iPhone, when it opens, tap Share, then Save to Files.
  3. Back in the app, tap Choose the PDF and pick the file you saved. It takes a few seconds, and stays on your phone.

On a lesser feast, the day is named for it and its collect becomes the Collect of the Day, in Rite One or Rite Two, with the Prayer Book’s collect as the other choice.

Getting started

  • The diocese’s cycles are already there: each day brings that day’s church and its clergy from the Diocesan Cycle, and the name for the date from the Diocesan Daily Cycle. When the diocese publishes next year’s daily cycle, add it from Lists → Diocesan Daily Cycle → Paste a list (or choose the PDF there).
  • Tap + to add a prayer request, for a day, a week, a month, or until you take it off.
  • Tap the circle beside a name once you’ve prayed, or use Pray through the list to go one at a time. On someone’s card there, Reach out puts them on a list of people to get in touch with, at the end of Today.
  • In Settings, choose Rite One (traditional) or Rite Two (contemporary) language, and pick prayers from the Prayer Book’s Prayers and Thanksgivings.

Your list stays yours

  • It’s free, with no account and no sign-in.
  • Everything you add stays on your phone; nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Because it’s kept only on your phone, save a backup now and then (Settings → Backup), and before you change phones. Removing the app, or clearing Safari’s website data, deletes your list.
  • New versions arrive by themselves.

The Diocesan Cycle and the Diocesan Daily Cycle are the Episcopal Diocese of Wisconsin’s, as it publishes them.

The prayers and collects are from the Book of Common Prayer (1979), which is in the public domain. The Anglican Cycle of Prayer is © the Anglican Consultative Council and is shared free of charge under its terms.
